A Fragile World Above the Trees

Summit Lake Park sits on the north face of Mount Blue Sky in a zone of alpine tundra that has more in common with northern Alaska than the surrounding Rockies. It is a disjunct ecosystem — a pocket of arctic habitat isolated thousands of miles from the Arctic Circle — and many of its species exist nowhere else on Earth.

Today, this ecosystem faces compounding threats: rising temperatures driven by climate change, increasing recreational pressure from the Denver metro area, invasive species encroachment, and degradation of the Bear Creek watershed that originates here.

This proposal outlines a science-based, community-supported conservation plan to ensure that Summit Lake Park remains a thriving alpine ecosystem for generations to come.

The species found here are more typical of northern Alaska than the rest of the Rockies, and many are endemic to this one location. This isolation makes this spectacular ecosystem very fragile.
